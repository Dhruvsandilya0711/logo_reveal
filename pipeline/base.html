Draft treatmentLit

  • Every face gets the same light

    All three face directions sit at one brightness, so nothing tells your eye which way each one turns. Give them three clear values: top faces brightest, left faces darkest, right faces lit by a coloured rim.

  • The glow is on every edge

    Neon on all edges draws an outline, and outlines read as flat graphics. Keep bright highlights for edges that face a light and let the rest fall into shadow.

  • The edges are hard cuts

    Real objects have rounded edges that catch a thin line of light. A small bevel gives every edge a highlight and makes the beams feel thick.

  • It floats in nothing

    No contact shadow, no floor, no light spill. A soft reflection and a warm light that touches the faces give the object a place in the world.

  • Detail runs across every face

    Circuits and the face profile cross all three face directions, which merges them into one surface. Put detail on one face family only.

  • It mixes two palettes

    Cyan to indigo to fuchsia is the sweep Coastal Night was made to replace. Either stay on teal, deep water and shallows, or commit to the neon palette everywhere, as the Ultraviolet direction does.

Renders are for big moments

Site hero, stage LED, posters, social launch. Anything smaller than about 160 px, printed on fabric, engraved or embroidered uses the flat tri-tone or mono.

The three tones never swap

Top faces Teal Lift, right faces Sea Teal, left faces Deep Water. The same order in the flat mark and the lit renders keeps them recognisably one object.

Amber belongs to the gap

In every version the warm light sits in the centre gap and nowhere else, matching the palette rule that amber is used once. It is where the loop closes and where the eye lands first.

Taking a direction to final

Blender, for the official files and the teaser

Exact geometry, full control, and the only route that can animate the lock.

  1. Model three square beams with a 3–4% bevel along X, Y and Z, joined end to end into an open path.
  2. Add an orthographic camera looking down the diagonal between the two free ends. Nudge it until the ends overlap and the triangle closes.
  3. Load the tri-tone SVG as the camera background and match the silhouette to it.
  4. Build the direction’s material and lights from the spec above. Put a small amber area light inside the centre gap.
  5. Render a 4K still, then a 6-second camera move from loose beams into the lock.

An image model, for fast mood boards

Good for exploring finishes in an afternoon. Not for the final logo file.

  1. Upload your black-and-white line drawing as the reference image.
  2. Paste the prompt from the direction you like and generate 8 to 12 results.
  3. Keep only results where the geometry matches. Overlay the tri-tone SVG at 50% opacity to check.
  4. Use the winners as a brief for the Blender render or for your designer.
